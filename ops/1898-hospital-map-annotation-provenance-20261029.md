# 1898 hospital-map image: source-object and annotation audit (2026-10-29 PHT)

**Research-only; not a published correction, a verified hospital event, or a new map feature.** The original WordPress article and all migrated article text/images remain unchanged. This audit does not authorize any new image reproduction or the previously safety-rejected CS12 operation CHM-B040.

## Direct inspection of the public article's existing figure

- Public article: [Cultural Significance No. 5](https://casahaciendamakati.wordpress.com/2023/10/21/cultural-significance-no-5/), dated 2023-10-21. Its inline figure links to the already-published `https://casahaciendamakati.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/cs-5.1.jpg?w=1024`.
- **Observed in that published figure, not inferred from its caption:** A beige street/river base map carries conspicuous red English labels, hospital-cross symbols and a large red heading, “SPANISH MILITARY AND FIELD HOSPITALS IN AND AROUND MANILA, MAY 1898.” A red label reads “House of Pedro Roxas in San Pedro Macati.” The red symbology and English annotations appear graphically superimposed on a different-looking cartographic base.
- **Critical provenance gap:** The article and visible image alone do not identify who made the red annotations, when they were made, which archival documents substantiate each site, or whether “May 1898” dates the base map, an underlying hospital plan, or a later historical reconstruction. A modern-looking annotation layer is **not itself** a contemporaneous Spanish military record. This is a visual/source-status inference, not a forensic determination of editing date.
- **Claim separation:** The figure presents a **suitability/identified site** claim in the article text. It does not independently establish that a field hospital actually opened at the House of Pedro Roxas, that it occupied the surviving Olympia building, or that the house at the indicated symbol was on the modern 9126 Sultana parcel. No claim of actual operations should be inferred from a marked symbol alone.

## Independent 1898 map candidates, not yet matched to the figure

- [WorldCat record for Francisco J. de Gamoneda, *Plano de Manila y sus arrabales* (1898)](https://search.worldcat.org/title/Plano-de-Manila-y-sus-arrabales/oclc/855805066), publisher Imprenta Litográfica de Ramón Montes, identifies a contemporary Spanish-language city map. [University of Texas Perry–Castañeda collection provenance, via NCpedia](https://www.ncpedia.org/media/map/manila-philippines-and) also describes an 1898 1:10,000 Gamoneda/Ramón Montes map.
- [1898 Norris Peters edition](https://www.geographicus.com/P/AntiqueMap/Manila-norrispeters-1898), a secondary dealer's bibliographic account, says a map was reproduced for the American major general's report, derived from Gamoneda. This is a **different edition**, not proof of the article figure's exact base.
- Neither candidate has been compared pixel-for-pixel with the unannotated base beneath `cs-5.1.jpg`. **Do not identify the figure's base as Gamoneda or Norris Peters yet**, and do not infer the red hospital overlay was printed in 1898. Source map provenance and rights require separate checking.

## Separate church-hospital photographic evidence

The LOC [2012648150](https://www.loc.gov/item/2012648150/), Smithsonian [NMAH.AC.0143_ref24224](https://www.si.edu/es/object/archives/components/sova-nmah-ac-0143-ref24224) and Ortigas Foundation indexed U250/SV-00044 records concern a *church at San Pedro Macati used as a hospital*, with circa-1899 publication/copyright metadata. They do **not** authenticate the article's red-annotated House of Pedro Roxas map, show an Olympia Casa Hacienda hospital, or independently date a hospital event. See `ops/church-hospital-stereoview-provenance-20261029.md`.

## Archival verification queue and acceptance limits

1. Obtain the creator/first publication of the red annotated hospital-site overlay and its cited underlying hospital roster, dispatch, plan, order, or map; verify source date, military administration, and “proposed” versus “operational.”
2. Identify the original map plate and compare the visible streets, river bend, labels, scale and orientation against Gamoneda 1898, Norris Peters 1898 and other editions. Record the exact repository, sheet/edition, catalogue ID and rights.
3. Verify whether the annotated “House of Pedro Roxas” symbol refers to Poblacion, Olympia, or another parcel using independent contemporary land and survey records. Do not assign the 1910/1926 photographs without accession captions.
4. Do not publish a map derivative, add a verified 1898 event, or correct the historical article until independently inspected sources and appropriate rights support the change. No paid access, WordPress write or new media authorized.

## Separate source transcription caution

The [NHCP's direct 1991 Makati marker registry](https://philhistoricsites.nhcp.gov.ph/registry_database/makati/) and multiple marker photographs transcribe the encomienda year **1608**. The NCCA's [TALAPAMANA Metro Manila aggregation](https://www.talapamana.ncca.gov.ph/index.php/component/content/article/talapamana-metro-manila?Itemid=101&catid=12) displays **1609** in a corresponding derivative entry. Treat this as a source-transcription discrepancy pending institutional clarification, **not** a reason to silently replace the direct marker's 1608 date. The existing timeline expressly attributes 1608 to the NHCP marker and remains unchanged.
