# Hospital-site and photograph identity: source-critical review (2026-10-29 PHT)

**Research only. No published-article correction, image reuse, rights clearance, or new site feature.** This record is a source-identity check, not evidence that the Olympia and Poblacion buildings are the same or that either was a particular military hospital.

## Distinct claims that must not be conflated

| Record | Directly observed / documented | What remains unverified |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Original public Cultural Significance No. 5 (2023) | The project argues that a map identifies a **Casa / House of Pedro Roxas** in San Pedro Macati as suitable for a **Spanish military field hospital in 1898**. Its own article says it does not know whether the property was volunteered or requisitioned. | The original map's creator, title, sheet, legend, date, archival location and coordinates; whether proposed use became actual use; whether that site was the surviving Olympia structure or the Poblacion offices. |
| Library of Congress item **2012648150** | The LOC catalogue title identifies **“Church at San Pedro Macati, used as a hospital, Philippine Islands”**, published by Strohmeyer & Wyman, **c1899**. The description identifies a stereograph of a man before a **church** in Makati; Underwood & Underwood is listed as copyright claimant. | Exact date the exposure was taken, the historical hospital's operators, any relation to the 1898 Spanish military map, and any link to the Casa Hacienda building or Olympia parcel. |
| FHL catalogue references **/biblio/18057/** and **/biblio/18040/** | The public original project's About page attributes its **1910 Casa Principal** and **1926 Casa Hacienda** photographs to the Filipinas Heritage Library. These are references in the project, not independently verified FHL object records. | Original accession metadata, captions and revisions, camera position, building identity, rights and reproduction permissions. Both catalogue object pages remained inaccessible for independent review on 2026-10-29. |

**Interpretive boundary:** The LOC photograph's *catalogue title* supports that a church was described as used as a hospital around the period of its c1899 publication. It does **not** verify or refute the separate project's 1898 assertion about the Casa de Pedro Roxas. A church, a Casa Hacienda, and a field-hospital candidate are three different identification questions. Do not treat one photograph as proof of another structure's use. Likewise, a c1899 publication date is not automatically the date of the depicted event.
