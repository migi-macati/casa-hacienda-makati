# Casa Quinta sanitary-model and orphanage claims: primary-source audit
Research-only, 2026-10-31 PHT. No changes to published articles, original WordPress, four private drafts, 82 media, PDFs, rights, or noindex. No new verified timeline entry or identification of the surviving Olympia building.

## Targeted historical question
Published *Cultural Significance No. 8* (https://casahaciendamakati.wordpress.com/2023/10/21/cultural-significance-no-7-2/) attributes a government orphanage and sanitary-house construction to Casa Quinta, San Pedro Macati, in 1917. It supplies precise start/completion dates, an international visitor, and a claim that Casa Quinta and the superintendent's quarters were the second and fourth Philippine model sanitary houses. These claims deserve primary-source verification before use in the new heritage timeline.

## Sources and provenance checked
1. The 1916 Philippine Bureau of Health report exists as the digitized University of Michigan / Internet Archive volume `acw9791.1916.001.umich.edu`, with a 262-page scan catalogued at https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Report_(IA_acw9791.1916.001.umich.edu).pdf . This confirms the volume identity, **not** the alleged 1917 works. A reporting period ending in 1916 cannot alone substantiate construction starting in January 1917; distinguish report period, publication year, and event date. Original relevant pages not inspected.
2. **Primary government report directly verified on 31 October 2026:** *Report of the Philippine Health Service for the Fiscal Year from January 1 to December 31, 1917*, J. D. Long, director, **published Manila: Bureau of Printing, 1918**. The original title page and Edward L. Barber's *Report of the Office of Sanitary Engineering*, **printed pp. 153–154 (PDF page indexes 154–155)** were inspected in the digitized original at https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Report_(IA_acw9791.1917.001.umich.edu).pdf (original PDF: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/6b/Report_%28IA_acw9791.1917.001.umich.edu%29.pdf ). Barber says his office assumed charge **15 January 1917** of reconstructing an existing large Spanish building called Casa Quinta in San Pedro Makati for the Government Orphanage, including major alteration, plumbing, septic tank, deep well, pump and tanks; the reconstruction was **completed 18 February 1917**. Separately, the office began construction of the **superintendent's quarters 23 April**, completed **4 June 1917**. These are contemporaneous official statements about the work, now directly verified at the cited pages, not independent proof of present-day building identity. The original published article mislabels this as a **1916 reporting year** in one citation, and prints `February 18, 197`; the original government report resolves the event year to **1917**. No article text was silently edited.
3. Philippine Health Service Bulletin No. 16, *Plans and Instructions Relative to the Construction of the Sanitary Model House* (1917), is cited at https://quod.lib.umich.edu/p/philamer/ACC3586.0001.016/1 . It is a lead for design specifications, **not independent proof of a particular surviving Makati building**. Original pages uninspected.
4. **Contemporaneous report inspected (new evidence, 31 October 2026):** *The Manning Times* (Manning, South Carolina), **17 October 1917, page 9**, article headed **“Many Inquiries About Philippine Model House”**. The University of South Carolina's digitized newspaper page exposes the contemporary printed article through OCR text at https://historicnewspapers.sc.edu/lccn/sn86063760/1917-10-17/ed-1/seq-9/ ; the original article also links the Library of Congress copy https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn86063760/1917-10-17/ed-1/?sp=9 . The newspaper reports that **K. Sogowa, described as a consulting engineer of Osaka Iron Works in Chohu Bakan, Japan, had been delegated by the Formosan government**, and had visited a **model house under construction at the government orphanage at Casa Quinta, San Pedro Macati** as well as the tuberculosis ward at San Lazaro Hospital. It reports international inquiries, rather than documentary proof that every named foreign government dispatched a representative. This independently corroborates the *1917 newspaper report* of a construction project at Casa Quinta and the reported visit, but the OCR is visibly imperfect. On 31 October 2026 the **original page-image facsimile was independently inspected**, using the archive's one-page PDF at https://historicnewspapers.sc.edu/lccn/sn86063760/1917-10-17/ed-1/seq-9.pdf (page 9; lower central newspaper column, below the shipping-fire report). The headline, K. Sogowa name as printed, Formosan delegation, and Casa Quinta government-orphanage construction reference are legible in the scan. The PDF was inspected read-only, not copied or republished. The newspaper is evidence that the report appeared by 17 October 1917; the word *recently* does not date Sogowa's visit. A syndication/printing lag is possible and has not been measured. The article is a newspaper report, not a construction contract, measured survey, official completion certificate, or proof that the surviving Olympia structure is the one discussed. It does not substantiate the claimed January/February or April/June dates, the second/fourth ordinals, or the building's present identity.

## Chronology caution after facsimile inspection
The October 1917 article refers to a model house under construction at the orphanage, whereas **Barber's official 1917 report now directly verifies** the existing Casa Quinta building's alteration was completed in February and the separate superintendent's quarters in June 1917. These descriptions may concern **different structures or construction stages**, and the newspaper's unspecified reporting lag prevents a direct contradiction from being inferred. Do not assign the October work to the surviving Casa building or claim the February/June dates were disproved. Require original plans, scope descriptions and dated engineering pages before reconciling the chronology.

## Consolidated primary-source and architectural-context findings (31 October 2026, evening)

The following material was independently researched during the day and first preserved in PR #135's conversation. This section consolidates the **source-specific findings into the versioned research dossier**. It does not change published articles or assert that the modern Olympia remains are the 1917 Casa Quinta.

### Government Orphanage, 1921: site and population

**Primary facsimile:** Office of the Public Welfare Commissioner, *The Government Orphanage*, Dependent Children Series No. 1 (Manila: Bureau of Printing, 1921), scanned 30-page pamphlet, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Government_orphanage_.._(IA_governmentorphan00philrich).pdf (Internet Archive identifier `governmentorphan00philrich`). Printed p. 7 (PDF index 10) was inspected in the original scan. The pamphlet says the Government Orphanage was established at **Casa Quinta, Makati, on the Pasig River**, in early 1917, occupying a **leased site of approximately two hectares**. It describes an **old residence remodeled for the orphanage, three additional buildings, and a schoolhouse**. The first **60 girls** arrived from Hospicio de San José on **12 February 1917**, six days before the Health Service report's stated **18 February** completion of the Casa Quinta reconstruction; occupancy and construction may have overlapped, but the arrangement is **not documented** by the two sources alone. The pamphlet mentions 75 later residents, 41 additional arrivals during 1919–20 and **226 residents in 1921**, excluding a separate Malate boys' home of 26; these are different reporting categories and must not be added without checking their time periods. Printed pp. 12–14 describe an orphanage hospital and voluntary religious instruction; this is **not evidence of the site's alleged 1898 military-hospital use**.

**Parcel caution:** Approximately two hectares (~20,000 square metres) in the 1921 pamphlet is a *leased institutional site*, not a measured footprint. It cannot be equated without cadastral or lease evidence to the **8,430-square-metre 1907 court parcel**, the published article's **8,600-square-metre** figure, or the **6,795-square-metre 2009 Olympia resolutions**. No owner, lease counterparty, parcel boundaries, or present-day building match has been verified. In particular, the original article's extrapolation to 600 beneficiaries is **not established by the 1921 census figures**.

### Legislative provenance: acts authorize a programme, not a parcel identification

**Primary official statutory text inspected:** Philippine **Act No. 2671**, approved **10 January 1917**, in the Supreme Court E-Library. Its charitable appropriation of **up to ₱150,000** covers multiple welfare purposes including orphans; it does **not** specify Casa Quinta, Don Pedro P. Roxas, a lease, an estate parcel, or an allocation of the entire sum to the orphanage. **Act No. 2815**, approved **4 March 1919**, reorganizes the Government Orphanage established in accordance with Act 2671 as the **Bureau of Dependent Children**; it does not identify the Makati site. Official Gazette index leads: University of San Carlos, *Index to Official Gazette (1913–1928)*, https://youseemore.com/san_carlos/uploads/Official%20Gazette%201913-1928.pdf , Act 2671 at **15(15):615 (11 April 1917)** and Act 2815 at **17(18):778 (30 April 1919)**. The printed Gazette pages themselves **have not been inspected**. Keep enacted approval dates separate from Gazette issue dates and from conflicting catalogue metadata. Do not confuse 1917 Philippine Act 2671 with the later Philippine Republic Act 2671.

### Different sanitary works, same reporting year

The **1917 Health Service annual report**, Barber pp. 153–154, distinguishes: a **new Carnival demonstration model house** constructed **12–26 January 1917**; the **San Lazaro tuberculosis hospital** works (15 January–14 April); **reconstruction of the existing Spanish Casa Quinta** (15 January–18 February); and **new orphanage superintendent's quarters** (23 April–4 June). The descriptions alone **do not number** Casa Quinta as the second model house or the quarters as the fourth. The October newspaper's reference to a house still *under construction* is not proof that Barber's February/June completion dates were wrong: the specific structure and newspaper reporting lag remain unverified. Printed pp. 160–165 of the same official report describe the Carnival demonstration-house programme separately from the Casa Quinta reconstruction. The director's summary gives **149,008** Carnival visitors whereas the detailed account gives **149,908**; do not silently reconcile the discrepancy. Printed p. 161 mentions **Santiago Jimenez of San Pedro Macati** as the kiln operator who fired vitrified-clay pipes for the Carnival model-house demonstration. This is a documented local industrial lead, **not evidence** connecting his kiln to the Casa Hacienda, a 1903 porcelain factory, or the Olympia property.

### Bulletin No. 16 and scholarly interpretation

**Primary bibliographic record:** Philippine Health Service, *Plans and Instructions Relative to the Construction of the Sanitary Model House* (Manila: Bureau of Printing, **1917**), Bulletin No. 16. UP Diliman Main Library Filipiniana catalogue https://www.elib.gov.ph/details.php?uid=2542e4aa5155880a2103eca495b3acc8 records **45 pages**, **English/Spanish/Tagalog**, call number **TH 6031 P5**, control **UPD-00000266809**, copies **FI-54759/FI-1824P**. The catalogue reports no digital copy **in that record**. However, the original published *Cultural Significance No. 8* already links a historical University of Michigan digital collection URL (`ACC3586.0001.016`, https://quod.lib.umich.edu/p/philamer/ACC3586.0001.016/1 ); that link was inaccessible during this run. Therefore do **not** assert that no digital scan exists anywhere. **Neither the 45 pages nor a complete authenticated digital facsimile have been inspected.** A catalogue entry is not proof of the original article's second/fourth model-house ranking.

**Secondary architectural context:** Gerard Lico, *Arkitekturang Filipino: A History of Architecture and Urbanism in the Philippines* (University of the Philippines Press, **2008**), ISBN **9789715425797**, published book independently catalogued by UP Diliman https://tuklas.up.edu.ph/Record/UP-1685594773861741494 and the publisher's distributor https://uhpress.hawaii.edu/title/arkitekturang-filipino-a-history-of-architecture-and-urbanism-in-the-philippines/ . The chapter on sanitary architecture (pp. **220–221** in searchable book excerpts; pagination should be checked against an authorized edition before quoting) discusses Bulletin No. 16 as a **standardized domestic house prototype**, with cement/rice-husk roofing, sawali reinforcement, spatial divisions and an Antipolo toilet. This **secondary typological discussion** strengthens the need to distinguish the *new standardized model house* from an *existing Spanish residence retrofitted with sanitary plumbing*. It does **not** establish which model house was second/fourth, the Casa Quinta construction materials, or a present-day building identity. No book images or substantial text reproduced.

### Claim classification for editorial and heritage use

- **Directly documented in primary sources:** 1917 Casa Quinta reconstruction and separate quarters with official work dates; a leased orphanage site at Casa Quinta and initial 60 admissions; the government charitable programme; contemporary newspaper reporting of Sogowa's visit; Santiago Jimenez's kiln as a Carnival-house supply source.
- **Requires qualification:** the 1921 pamphlet's approximate two-hectare leased site and year-specific resident counts; the October 1917 newspaper's unspecified construction stage; historical model-house materials discussed by later scholarship.
- **Unverified or unsupported by sources inspected:** the second/fourth ordinal; identification of the surviving Olympia building; equivalence of 20,000 / 8,600 / 8,430 / 6,795 square metres; identity of lessor or landowner for the 1917 lease; link between Jimenez and the porcelain factory; 1898 military-hospital use; a 600-child capacity; full extent of international inspection visits.
