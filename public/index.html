The place

The Casa Hacienda in Olympia

At Sultana and J.B. Roxas Streets in Barangay Olympia, surviving remains connect Makati’s modern cityscape with its hacienda past. This research project explores the house’s documented history, architecture and cultural significance.

Evidence-led chronology

Early San Pedro Macati: what the records date

This first verified segment concerns the wider San Pedro Macati estate, church and novitiate. It does not establish when the surviving structure in Barangay Olympia was built or prove that the institutions occupied the same building. Later periods require separate archival verification.

  1. 1607 · Buenavista donation

    The historical marker installed in 1937 records Captain Pedro de Brito's donation of the Buenavista site and his endowment of the Jesuit novitiate. The marker refers to the church and novitiate precinct, not the surviving Olympia building.

    Source: San Pedro Macati historical marker, 1937 (NHCP).

  2. 1608 · Encomienda reference

    A different historical marker, installed in 1991, dates the grant of an encomienda to Pedro de Brito to 1608. This describes a different act from the 1607 site donation; the dates should not be treated as interchangeable.

    Source: Makati historical marker, 1991 (NHCP).

  3. 1620 · Jesuit church

    The 1937 marker dates construction of the San Pedro Macati church by the Jesuits to 1620. This is a church-construction date, not a documented construction date for the Casa Hacienda.

    Source: San Pedro Macati historical marker (NHCP).

  4. 1768 · End of Jesuit church administration

    The 1937 marker records Jesuit administration of the church through 1768. It does not establish the precise date of transfer or later ownership of the Casa Hacienda.

    Source: San Pedro Macati historical marker (NHCP).

  5. 1769 · Jesuit expulsion preparations continued

    Historian Santiago Lorenzo García traces the Philippine Jesuit expulsion operations across 1768–1771, with definitive preparations for expatriation in 1769. This wider colonial chronology does not establish when the Makati hacienda changed ownership or when the surviving Olympia house was built.

    Source: Santiago Lorenzo García, La expulsión de los Jesuitas de Filipinas (1999), published contents, chapter IV.

The first four dates are reported by later historical markers, not original deeds or building plans. The 1769 entry follows a scholarly publication record; neither is a deed or a verified construction date. For the evidence concerning the surviving house, read the site-location research and published cultural-significance studies.

Research evidence

Sources and what they establish

These institutional records help date parts of San Pedro Macati's history. The record date, the event described and the surviving Casa Hacienda building are distinct questions. This register lists checked source identities, including evidence that still requires direct examination.

  1. 1937 historical marker: San Pedro Macati

    Record: A later commemorative inscription reporting a 1607 site donation, church construction in 1620 and Jesuit administration through 1768.

    Limit: The marker is not the original deed or a construction record for the surviving house in Olympia.

    Read the NHCP marker record

  2. 1991 historical marker: Makati

    Record: A separate inscription dating Pedro de Brito's encomienda grant to 1608.

    Limit: An encomienda grant is a different act from the 1607 donation; this inscription does not establish the surviving house's age.

    Read the NHCP Makati record

  3. 1775 manuscript map: Manila environs

    Record: The British Library catalogue identifies a map attributed to Diono. Kelley as Add MS 17641 C, record 040-002027896.

    Limit: The map sheet has not been examined. Its catalogue date does not establish that the Olympia house appears on the map or existed in 1775. No map image is reproduced here.

    Read the British Library catalogue record

  4. 1999 historical study: Jesuit expulsion in the Philippines

    Record: The University of Alicante publication by Santiago Lorenzo García treats expulsion operations from 1768 to 1771, including preparations for expatriation in 1769.

    Limit: The publisher record and table of contents were checked; the monograph’s full text and original estate records were not examined. This does not identify a Makati parcel, a transfer deed, or the surviving Olympia building.

    Read the published study record

Location and identity

Two Casa Hacienda locations in the historical accounts

The published Olympia research and an independent Poblacion heritage-planning account describe different locations associated with the name Casa Hacienda. Their building identities, chronology and archival photographs must be assessed separately.

  1. Olympia · Sultana and J.B. Roxas Streets

    Published project claim: The original research locates surviving remains at 9126 Sultana Street, Barangay Olympia. Its Cultural Significance No. 3 expressly distinguishes this location from the better-known Casa Hacienda associated with Poblacion Park.

    Evidence limit: The claim is the project's interpretation of photographs, maps and surviving fabric; the original archival photographs and the 1775 map sheet have not yet been independently verified against this parcel.

    Read the published Olympia location argument

  2. Poblacion · Pasig waterfront and Poblacion Park

    Independent secondary account: Heritage planner Leon M. Mayo describes a former wood-and-stone Casa Hacienda on the Pasig waterfront, now Poblacion Park, used as offices of Ayala y Compañía. His account appeared in BluPrint's 2011 special issue and online in 2018.

    Evidence limit: This account is not an original land survey, title or photograph accession record. It cannot establish that the surviving Olympia remains and the former Poblacion offices are the same building.

    Official city account: The Makati City Government's Barangay Poblacion page independently identifies Poblacion Park as the former Casa Hacienda site and offices of the precursor of Ayala Corporation. This confirms the city's description of the Poblacion location; it does not verify the Olympia building's identity.

    Read Makati City's Poblacion heritage listing

    Read the Poblacion heritage-planning account

This is a comparison of published location claims, not a verified parcel-level identification of either structure. The 1910 and 1926 archive photograph captions, subjects and reproduction rights remain unverified. No historical photograph, map image or location overlay is reproduced here. People and records

People named in the historical records

These records identify people associated with San Pedro Macati across different periods. A historical name or estate transaction does not establish who occupied, designed or built the surviving structure in Olympia.

  1. Pedro de Brito

    Recorded role: The National Historical Commission's 1937 San Pedro Macati marker attributes the Buenavista site donation and endowment of the Jesuit novitiate to Captain Pedro de Brito in 1607.

    Evidence limit: This is a later marker's account of the church and novitiate precinct. It is not a building record for the surviving Olympia structure.

    Read the NHCP historical marker

  2. Pedro P. Roxas

    Recorded role: The 1907 Supreme Court decision in Roxas v. Tuason recounts his February 19, 1906 application to register the Hacienda de San Pedro Macati. It describes a Casa-Quinta or Casa de Ingenieros in parcel C, but does not identify its modern location.

    Evidence limit: The application date is not a building-construction date. The 8,430-square-meter figure in the decision includes appurtenances and is not the house's footprint.

    Read a transcription of the 1907 court decision

  3. Julia Tuason

    Recorded role: The same decision identifies her opposition to the 1906 land-registration application concerning a disputed boundary of parcel C.

    Evidence limit: A boundary dispute does not establish that Tuason occupied or owned the Casa-Quinta building. The court record is not a verified survey match to the modern Olympia or Poblacion sites.

    Read a second transcription of the court decision

The court texts linked above are third-party transcriptions of a published 1907 decision (9 Phil. 408); the official report scan and 1906 parcel plan have not been inspected. These entries do not claim that the named people occupied the surviving house, and no portraits or archival images are reproduced.

Documented actions

Events in the estate's legal record

These two events are dated by a published Supreme Court decision concerning the Hacienda de San Pedro Macati. They describe a registration proceeding and a judicial decision, not the construction or ownership history of the surviving Olympia house.

  1. February 19, 1906 · Registration application

    Recorded event: The 1907 Supreme Court opinion in Roxas v. Tuason recounts Pedro P. Roxas's application to register the Hacienda de San Pedro Macati, described in four parcels A through D.

    Evidence limit: An application is not proof that a title was awarded, that a deed was executed, or that the Casa-Quinta in parcel C corresponds to the modern Olympia or Poblacion sites.

    Read the 1907 court-decision transcription

  2. December 21, 1907 · Roxas v. Tuason

    Recorded event: The Supreme Court issued its decision in G.R. No. L-3788, published at 9 Phil. 408, discussing a contested parcel C boundary in the hacienda registration proceeding.

    Evidence limit: The decision is not a building-construction record or a verified survey match to 9126 Sultana Street. The 8,430-square-meter description of the Casa-Quinta parcel includes appurtenances and is not the house's footprint.

    Read a second court-decision transcription

These links are third-party transcriptions of a judicial source. The official 1907 report scan and original 1906 parcel plan have not been inspected. The entries do not establish a completed land transfer, an 1898 military hospital, or the identity of the surviving house. No archival image or map is reproduced.
