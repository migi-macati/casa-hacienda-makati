Discover the Cultural Significance of the Casa Hacienda of Makati

The Casa Hacienda de San Pedro Macati still stands today. But why should we care? From centuries-old history to key roles in Philippine industry, education, and liberation, each chapter in the Casa Hacienda’s story adds to the rich fabric of Makati’s heritage. Let’s explore why this site matters deeply to past, present, and future generations of Makatizens and Filipinos.

Cultural Significance No. 1

Centuries of Resilience

A landmark that has withstood the tides of time, revolution, and modernization—Casa Hacienda has remained resilient for centuries, a testament to Makati’s enduring spirit.

Learn More

Cultural Significance No. 2

Architectural Heritage of the Bahay-na-Bato

Discover the grandeur of Spanish-Filipino design. Casa Hacienda exudes the elegance of the Bahay-na-Bato style, symbolizing an era of cultural fusion and opulence.

Learn More

Cultural Significance No. 3

A Prominent Location Marked for Over 200 Years

For over two centuries, Casa Hacienda has been a prominent landmark in Makati, appearing on historical maps as an anchor amidst the town's dynamic growth.

Learn More

Cultural Significance No. 4

The Embodiment of Hacienda Life in Makati

Step into the history of hacienda life—a culture that formed the roots of Makati’s development. Casa Hacienda is a living reminder of this era, embodying Makati’s agricultural past and urban aspirations.

Learn More

Cultural Significance No. 5

A Spanish Military Field Hospital

During the Philippine Revolution, Casa Hacienda served as a field hospital for Spanish forces, marking it as a site of both strategic importance and humanitarian care.

Learn More

Cultural Significance No. 6

Site of the First Philippine Porcelain Factory

Casa Hacienda hosted La Porcelanica, the first porcelain factory in the Philippines, setting a precedent for local industry and showcasing early Filipino craftsmanship.

Learn More

Cultural Significance No. 7

First Government Orphanage

As the nation transitioned from religious charity to social welfare, Casa Hacienda became home to the first government orphanage in the Philippines, providing a safe haven for children in need.

Learn More

Cultural Significance No. 8

One of the First Model Sanitary Houses (Bahay Huwaran)

The Insular Government showcased innovations in sanitation at Casa Hacienda, turning it into a Bahay Huwaran—an example of early efforts to improve health and living standards.

Learn More

Cultural Significance No. 9

Original Home of the Jose Fabella Memorial School

Dedicated to education, Casa Hacienda housed the original Jose Fabella Memorial School, playing a pioneering role in special education for Filipino children.

Learn More

Cultural Significance No. 10

Site of the Lissar Soap and Perfume Factory

In the early 20th century, Casa Hacienda became home to the renowned Lissar Soap and Perfume Factory, placing Makati at the heart of the burgeoning Philippine cosmetics industry.

Learn More

Cultural Significance No. 11

Ground Zero of the Liberation of Makati

Casa Hacienda witnessed Makati’s liberation during WWII, serving as a strategic site for U.S. forces. It stands as a reminder of the courage and resilience that shaped Makati’s path to freedom.

Learn More

Cultural Significance No. 12

It was part of the Philippine Sugar Empire.

Under the ownership of Victorias Milling Company, Casa Hacienda was a hub for the sugar industry—a powerful force in shaping both local and national economies during the early 20th century.

Learn More

Each of these cultural significances paints a unique part of Makati’s vibrant past and present. Dive deeper into our heritage. Follow us for more stories and updates on Casa Hacienda Makati’s ongoing journey.
