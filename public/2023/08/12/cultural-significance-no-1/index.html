Cultural Significance No. 1

Originally published 2023-08-12

The Casa Hacienda of Makati is Centuries Old.

The Casa Hacienda of Makati holds a unique position in the city's history, standing as one of the oldest structures in the area. Though the exact date of its construction remains uncertain, historical sources indicate that the building could be between 100 to over 400 years old. Its longstanding presence reflects a rich legacy of resilience, adaptation, and cultural importance.

The Origins: A Jesuit Foundation in 1607

In 1607, Don Pedro de Brito and Doña Ana de Herrera founded Hacienda San Pedro Macati, donating it to the Jesuits to support the formation of Jesuit novices in the Philippines. Given that the Jesuits established similar "casa haciendas" across their various estates to house administrators and store agricultural goods, it is highly probable that a structure was established on this site early on, possibly as part of their broader mission and administrative needs.1

1769: Expulsion of the Jesuits

The Jesuits were expelled from Spanish colonies in 1769, and the Makati hacienda was seized by the Spanish Crown. By the time Don Pedro de Galarraga, the Marquis of Villamediana, acquired the estate in 1795, records suggest the presence of an "excelente casa" that required significant repairs. This implies that the Casa Hacienda structure, or at least its foundations, existed prior to 1769, marking its importance even in those early years.2

1775: Earliest Known Map Evidence

A 1775 map of Manila by D. Kelly provides the earliest cartographic evidence of the Casa Hacienda’s location, marking it as a prominent feature in the landscape. This early depiction underlines the estate's significant role in Makati's geography and suggests that the Casa Hacienda was already a well-known landmark by the late 18th century.3

1910: The Oldest Photograph of the Casa Hacienda

The oldest known photograph of the Casa Hacienda dates back to 1910. This historic image captures the building in its original form, showcasing its architectural beauty and historical significance. Remarkably, this is the same structure whose remnants can still be seen today at 9126 Sultana St., Brgy Olympia, Makati City.4

2021: Enduring Yet Neglected

In 2021, the remains of the Casa Hacienda were rediscovered, albeit in a state of neglect and disrepair, hidden behind a newly constructed city warehouse. This site remains a tangible reminder of Makati’s layered history, standing testament to centuries of change, resilience, and transformation. Efforts to preserve and restore the Casa Hacienda would not only safeguard its structural legacy but also ensure that its stories continue to inspire future generations.
