Cultural Significance No. 3

Originally published 2023-08-12

The Casa Hacienda of Makati is built in a very prominent location.

The Casa Hacienda of Makati is built in a very prominent location that became the town's center of industry and commerce. The Casa Hacienda is marked intently on maps for more than 200 years.

Tracing the solitary road (now J.P. Rizal Ave.) to San Pedro Macati from his mother Santa Ana, the figurative umbilical chord is cut almost in half by a dedicated road (now J.B. Roxas St.) that leads to the Casa Hacienda, the large bahay-na-bato by the Pasig River.

The dedicated road was eventually extended to provide the Casa Hacienda its own tranvia station along the Pasig Line (now Kalayaan Ave.), the rail line from Manila to Pasig. A local trading and commercial center thrived at the intersection.

The site is accessible by land, and, more importantly, by the river - the safer route to ferry passengers and goods until the domination of automobiles in the mid-20th century.

In full 360-degrees, the Casa Hacienda was surrounded by the economic activities of the hacienda. Until after WW2, the surroundings were mostly farms and grazing lands. On the shores of the river were brick and tile kilns and potteries, capitalizing on the suitable clay extracted on site. Later on, porcelain will also be produced on site, but only for a short while. These locally produced products would have been stored in the Casa Hacienda before being transported to be sold in Manila.

As far back as 1826, we see rows of houses probably of tenant-workers along the dedicated road. (These areas would rightly be called Trabajo, Obrero, Hinyero, and Olympia after name of the tile and brick factory.) These tenant-workers would have been migrants from other parts of the colony that were looking for work to provide for their families, which they also brought with them. They would have had to report to the hacienda administrator and pay their rents at the Casa Hacienda.

The site's prominence started its decline when the riverbanks became prime locations for newer factories right before WW2. The owners of the hacienda also developed the farmlands and sold them off as low-income subdivisions.

Today, the site's vicinity is densely populated and mostly residential.

To avoid any doubt, the site's exact location is at 9126 Sultana St., Brgy. Olympia, Makati City. It is notable that the more commonly known casa hacienda of Makati in what is now Poblacion Park does not appear, or at least not as prominently, in any of the maps.
