Cultural Significance No. 2

Originally published 2023-08-12

The Casa Hacienda of Makati showcases a unique Bahay-na-Bato style, exuding grandeur and influence.

Through the two building photographs taken in a century ago, we are able to appreciate the architectural and aesthetic significance of the Casa Hacienda of Makati at it's height in 1910s, and it's deterioration in the 1920s.

To appreciate its grandeur, its massiveness is comparable to the Nuestra Señora de Gracia Church and Nielson Tower, both in Makati, the Casa Manila in Intramuros, and the Lazi Convent (National Cultural Treasure) in Siquijor.

The structure has undergone several documented renovations for its various uses through time.

The original Jesuit casa hacienda building has been damaged by natural and artificial disasters through time and was repaired by the Marquis of Villamediana upon acquisition of the hacienda in 1785.

The earliest known photo of the structure is dated 1910. From then, a photo of the structure taken in 1926 shows that the tiled roof has been replaced with galvanized sheets, and the general structure and its surrounding had deteriorated.

The structure’s documented renovations and improvements made for its use as the first Government Orphanage in the Philippines. The structure was further renovated for use as the corporate office and staff housing of Victorias Milling Co., which is the last use for the property before being acquired and used by the City Government of Makati as a City Warehouse.

In 2018, the structure had burned down for unknown reasons, leaving the masonry exposed at present.
