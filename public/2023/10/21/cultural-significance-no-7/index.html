Cultural Significance No. 7

Originally published 2023-10-21

The Casa Hacienda of Makati was used to house the First Government Orphanage.

Before 1917, most social welfare services including caring for orphans were run by private organizations, usually religious. Most famous among these are Hospicio de San Jose and Asilo de San Vicente de Paul.

The First Government Orphanage is the embodiment of a town, a country in transition - from the Spanish colonial government to a government patterned after that of the Americans, from largely religious or charitable institutions to nonsectarian social welfare.

In 1917, the Insular government rented the Casa Hacienda, more commonly known at that time as the Casa Quinta and referred to as a “large Spanish building,” to establish the first welfare agency - the Government Orphanage. It might have been the government who assessed the site as suitable for this purpose or maybe it was the Ayalas, known for their philanthropy, who offered their underutilized property to the government.

What makes the Casa Hacienda suitable for use as an orphanage?

1. It has 8,600 sqm wide-open grounds perfect for play and the physical training of children.

Activities that made suitable use of the grounds include calisthenics, military drills, physical contests, and training activities such as running, jumping, indoor baseball, boxing, and other forms of athletics. Some of the children were also assigned to do the gardening and groundskeeping work.

2. It has large facilities that were able to accommodate up to 240 children and a few personnel.

The top floor was used as dormitories while the ground floor was utilized for the kitchen, dining room, classrooms, and office. Orphans were grouped in dormitories according to size, age, and educational attainment. There are four dormitories in the Orphanage, one for big boys, one for big girls, one for small boys, and another for small girls. Infants taken care of in the institution are quartered in one of the rooms in the building used as a hospital. Such is the generosity of space that “moving picture performances” aka movie nights were held there using the same apparatus as in film houses.

3. It was available for rent.

As discussed in our previous post on the subject, the Casa Hacienda had to be remodeled for this new purpose, and subsequently, 3 other buildings were constructed in the complex. The orphanage was placed under the care of Dr. Jose F. Fabella, the father of Philippine Social Welfare, and predated the establishment of both the Bureau of Dependent Children in 1919 and the Office of the Public Welfare Commission in 1921. Dr. Fabella, as the head of the orphanage and both agencies, was living in the newly constructed superintendent’s quarters also within the complex.

There were on average about 50 graduating orphans each year from 1917 when the orphanage was established to 1926 when it was transferred to Welfareville in Mandaluyong. With a capacity to shelter more than 200 orphans, there would have been around 600 dependent children that benefited from the care in the Government Orphanage in Makati (Bonifacio Roxas Street, San Pedro Makati, Rizal).

This year 2022, the oldest surviving graduate would be 119 years old, entering the orphanage in 1917 at 14 years old, while the youngest could now be 96 years old, being admitted into the orphanage in 1926 at less than a year old.

Of course, those grown-up orphans would have had to survive a number of pandemics like the Spanish Flu and the Asian Flu, and worse, being conscripted or being victims of World War 2.
