Cultural Significance No. 9

Originally published 2023-10-21

The Casa Hacienda of Makati was is the original home of the the Jose Fabella Memorial School.

We have previously seen the Casa Hacienda of Makati used as a military field hospital, as an orphanage, and as a model sanitary house. Now, we explore its role in the education sector, specifically in special education (SPED).

Aiming to provide holistic care to orphans or dependent children, in 1917, Dr. Jose Fabella envisioned the orphanage to provide not only for their shelter, but for their healthcare and education as well. The instruction of orphans has the special purpose of preparing them for work and their economic independence upon graduation.

Named in honor of the Father of Social Work and the First Commissioner of the Office of Public Welfare, the Jose Fabella Memorial School was and still is the government’s primary response to address the special and additional educational needs of children, including among others those who are orphaned, abandoned, mentally handicaped, homeless, and those in conflict with the law. Today, it is one of the 4 government SPED schools, and comprises 12 SPED units located in various parts of the country (list provided below).

The school was previously called the Welfareville School when it transferred to its permanent location in the Welfareville Compound, Mandaluyong. Before that, it was known simply as the Orphanage School - being the only one in the country run by the government.

Back to 1917, the Orphanage School in the Casa Hacienda compound had a complete elementary school, catering to admitted children from the first to the seventh grade. It was initially managed by the Educational Division of the Bureau of Dependent Children. Initially, classes were held on the ground floor of the Casa Hacienda building itself, then a separate schoolhouse in the compound was constructed by 1921.

The work in this bureau being primarily educational, Mr. Honorio Poblador, a division superintendent of schools in the Bureau of Education, was appointed assistant director of the Bureau of Dependent Children on November 1, 1919. The organization and administration of the school was then patterned after that of the other public schools under the Bureau of Education.

Aside from the usual academic instruction, however, the Orphanage School placed extra emphasis on vocational training. Boys could choose a trade among carpentry, gardening, automobile driving, weaving, and barbering. Meanwhile, girls were trained in sewing, cooking, embroidery and housekeeping. Older boys were even sent off-campus to be apprentices in shops and other establishments in Manila, while some girls were sent to take up nursing and teaching in colleges.

The concept of educational facilities being run by the government at that time, or the practicality of having industrial courses in primary education might have confused those who saw the repurposed casa hacienda then. Dr. Fabella noted that the orphanage administration was kept busy by turning away many parents trying to admit their children, thinking that the institution was a college. Granted that these parents claimed they had no means to send their children to school, still, that is not how orphanages work. What kept them from sending their children to public schools that existed back then instead?

As mentioned in previous posts, on August 1, 1926, the Government Orphanage together with the Orphanage School vacated the Casa Hacienda of Makati, and completed the transfer to its current location in Welfareville, where three new and larger buildings were constructed for them.

Remember that the government was only renting the casa hacienda and it was thus returned to the hacienda’s owners in 1926. These were then Jacobo, Alfonso and Mercedes Zobel y Roxas, who inherited the hacienda from the estate of their grandfather, Don Pedro P. Roxas. Alfonso Zobel y Roxas is the grandfather of Jaime Augusto Zobel de Ayala (JAZA), the current Chairman and CEO of Ayala Corporation.

We would like to hear your thoughts about the contribution of the Casa Hacienda de San Pedro Macati to education. Next time, we’ll touch on the new role of the casa hacienda in the age of industrialists.
