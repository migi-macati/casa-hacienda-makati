Cultural Significance No. 10

Originally published 2023-10-21

The Casa Hacienda of Makati was the site of the world-renowned Lissar Soap and Perfume Factory.

From around 1936 to beyond 1952, the Casa Hacienda of Makati was used as the factory of the Lissar Compañia Incorporada while the Philippines entered into another round of rapid Industrialization. The modern factory manufactured soap, cosmetics, toilet articles, and high-end boutique perfumes on a large scale never before seen in the country.1 2

In the 1930s on the other hand, Ayala y Cia was facing severe cash flow problems and was forced to liquidate some of its assets, choosing to focus on select businesses such as banking, securities, rentals, and their newfound revenue stream, real estate development. The company sold its pharmaceuticals, its tramcar lines, its distillery business, the Ginebra San Miguel de Ayala, as well as large portions of Hacienda Makati by developing and selling them as subdivision lots.3 These included the subdivisions in the City of Manila - San Andres, San Andres Extension, and Singalong Extension - and those in Makati - Cristobal, Olimpia, Consuelo, and San Pedro Makati.4 Considering the conversion of the surrounding farms into residential subdivisions and the increasing notoriety in the area, the Casa Hacienda of Makati in Olimpia, which has just recently been vacated by its long-term tenant - the Government Orphanage - in 1926, was ripe for liquidation. [inference]

It would have been around this time that the Lizárragas from Iloilo and Negros Occidental through their merchant house, Lizárraga Hermanos, established and operated the Lissar retail outlets in Manila, Iloilo, and Cebu and the soap and perfume factory in Makati 5 to take advantage of the trade liberalization policies at that time. They bought the Casa Hacienda property from the Ayalas as they would found its size and location along the banks of the Pasig River suitable for the company’s operations. [inference] The plant facilities would eventually be composed of 5 main buildings with around 150 personnel, producing consumer goods of about 8,000 tons annually, and exporting to retail stores across the country and in Europe by the start of World War 2.6 Production was managed by a French chemist who oversaw the installation and use of machinery and equipment from France with raw materials imported also from France, and packaging from the US and local sources. The finished goods competed with French and American perfumes sold in the country and abroad.

Operations continued both in the Lissar Salon in the Calvo Building, Escolta, Manila, and in the factory in San Pedro Makati as the war in the Asia-Pacific started in 1941 and even during the Japanese occupation of the Philippines.7 Everything had to stop, however, as the Japanese Army requisitioned the factory at the height of the war with the impending liberation of Manila by the American Forces towards the end of 1944.8

The war took a large toll on the Lizárragas with the tragic death of their patriarch, Don Tirso Lizárraga y Fernández, Jr., during the 1945 Manila Massacre, and with the devastation to their businesses. This led the surviving heirs of Lizárraga Hermanos to sell Lissar Cia., Inc. to the Luzon Industrial Corp., headed by Don Jesús Amado "Amading" Sitchon Araneta (sugar baron and developer of Araneta Center in Cubao).9 The new owner and management quickly repaired and resumed operations of the factory, and worked to expand and further modernize its operations, aiming to raise its annual production to 15,000 tons. The Lissar factory in Makati would continue producing export-quality products into the import-substitution industrialization period of the Philippines well beyond 1952.10 That is before it ceases operations and sells off the Casa Hacienda compound to another sugar baron. [inference]

Today, fewer people could still remember the luxury exuding from the advertisements of Lissar perfumes and soaps, and fewer still are those that were able to use them. Some would remember the dance craze music segments sponsored by Lissar over the radio. And, others still remember the boutique Lissar Salon in the Calvo Building in Escolta. Lissar came, survived the war, and disappeared, but its legacy in the Philippine Industrialization period is forever etched in the stones of the remains of the Casa Hacienda of Makati that preceded and outlasted the Lissar Cia., Inc.

Note: Locating the Lissar factory within the Casa Hacienda property is corroborated by the addresses and description of the factory with the aerial photographs of the site11 12. Located at J.B. Roxas St., Barrio Olimpia, San Pedro Makati13 along the banks of the Pasig Riverand consisting of 5 main buildings, this pinpoints the current address of the factory at the Casa Hacienda of Makati, 9126 Sultana St., Brgy. Olympia, Makati City.
