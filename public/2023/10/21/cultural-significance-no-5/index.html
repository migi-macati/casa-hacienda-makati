Cultural Significance No. 5

Originally published 2023-10-21

The Casa Hacienda of Makati was used as a Spanish Military Field Hospital.

Marked as the Casa or House of Pedro Roxas in San Pedro Macati, the Casa Hacienda of Makati was identified as one of the strategic sites suitable for use as a field hospital.

This was during the re-escalating conflict with the Filipino revolutionaries and the beginning of the war with the Americans in 1898. The establishment of field hospitals became necessary not only to treat the injured and ill troops from the front lines, but also to alleviate the fully occupied permanent military hospitals in Manila.

It is often left unsaid but the fact is undeniable that the Spanish colonial army was from the very beginning largely composed of natives from Luzon and Visayas. The beneficiaries of the improved medical efforts of the Spanish military would then have naturally included Filipinos.

We do not know if Don Pedro Pablo Roxas volunteered the use of his property or if the Spanish military forcefully commissioned the casa hacienda.

What we do know is that the heads of the Roxas-Ayala-Zobel families. including Jose Bonifacio Roxas, Margarita Roxas, and Pedro Roxas, were widely known for their legacy of philanthropic and civic works that continues to benefit Filipinos to this day.

Finally, it is good to note that all the military and field hospitals marked in the map, except for the Malate Military Hospital (currently the site of the Land Bank of the Philippines Plaza) and the Casa Hacienda of Makati, had historical markers installed (although the marker for the Jesuit House in Santa Ana has been apparently stolen).
