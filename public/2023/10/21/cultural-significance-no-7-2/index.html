Cultural Significance No. 8

Originally published 2023-10-21

The Casa Hacienda of Makati was one of the first Philippine Model Sanitary Houses or Bahay Huwaran.

The Insular Government of the Philippine Islands had decided to rent the Casa Hacienda from the owner of the hacienda, Don Pedro P. Roxas, to be used as the first Government Orphanage. On 15 January 1917, works immediately began to retrofit the building, also called the Casa Quinta, to facilitate the care for children while taking advantage of the opportunity to showcase the latest innovations in sanitation.

During the US colonial period, the Office of Sanitary Engineering under the Bureau of Health was tasked to look for engineering solutions to improve the sanitary conditions in Filipino communities and households nationwide. With the beginning of the cholera and the typhoid epidemics and equipped with the new bacteriological frame of mind, they sought to update the common Filipino homes that are mostly made out of nipa. (Those who live in or use buildings of bahay-na-bato were mostly the rich and the influential institutions; the majority of Filipinos has continued to live in bahay kubo made of nipa palm leaves.)

The reconstruction works on the Casa Hacienda included “the entire alteration of the building, installation of modern plumbing fixtures and septic tank, and the installation of a deep well together with pump and tanks.” This was completed on February 18, 197. A few months after the reconstruction, on April 23, construction of a separate building for the Quarters of the Superintendent of the Government Orphanage was started, and was completed on June 4,1917.

International Attention

Even while under construction, the developments in the Casa Hacienda had attracted local and international attention for being one of the first and most renowned model sanitary houses in the region. Its renown became so widespread that foreign governments from Java and the Malacca Straits up to Japan, India and Puerto Rico sent representatives to inspect the model house in Makati first-hand.

One of these representatives, K. Sogowa of the Formosan government [Taiwan], “showed much interest in looking over the house under construction at the government orphanage at Casa Quinta, San Pedro Macati,” “praised the system for its durability, cheapness and good sanitation,” and recommended to the Formosan government to adopt it.

Bahay Huwaran

So what exactly did these foreign and local representatives see when they looked at the new “Model House”, “Bahay Huwaran'' or “Casa Sanitarias Modelo” style of architecture?

At the time of the reconstruction of the Casa Quinta and the construction of the superintendent’s quarters, there were two recent innovations that are characteristic of the Model House: 1) the modified Antipolo toilet system, and 2) the shingles and slabs made of a mixture of cement, sand, rice husk and bamboo for walls and roofing, invented in Mariveles the previous year.

The government's aim was to convince and enable the common Filipino, not just the rich, to convert their bahay kubo made of nipa to a ‘bahay kubo’ made of sanitary, more durable, and, arguably, more economical materials. (They would go as far as saying that it was more beautiful and attractive.) The goal was not so much as to promote an architectural style than to promote the American way of life or mindset that values practicality and efficiency.

The Casa Hacienda of Makati played a pioneering role in this national and global effort of modernization as the second (Casa Quinta) and fourth (superintendent’s quarters) model sanitary house ever to be built. (The first was the small model house constructed for the 1917 Carnival and was later transferred to San Lazaro Hospital.)

The Philippine Health Service would later go around the country promoting the adoption of the model sanitary house, bringing with them photos of germs and parasites that will be ingrained in Filipino’s consciousness. These developments would resound throughout the century and are still echoing today as the world faces the COVID-19 pandemic.
